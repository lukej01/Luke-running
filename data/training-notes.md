# Training notes

Narrative context. Numbers live in the CSVs. Newest first.

## 2026-08-27 — baseline set

Roughly a month at 45 miles/week, about three weeks of consistent workouts, no
active injury. Four symptom-free weeks on the board, so **the step to 48 is
earned**. 10 weeks to state.

Racing essentially every Saturday from here to state. That fixes the weekly
shape: the race is one hard day, so one workout midweek, Tuesday.

## The summer cross-training block

Roughly six weeks at **2.5 hours a day** of bike and aqua jogging while injured:

| | equivalents/week |
|---|---|
| 5 days/wk | 75 |
| 6 days/wk | 90 |
| 7 days/wk | 105 |

Against a 50-mile running ceiling. He carried close to double his running
ceiling in aerobic load, while injured, and it aggravated nothing.

**That splits the diagnosis in two.** His aerobic tolerance is enormous; his
impact tolerance caps at 50. Those are separate budgets and every prescription
should treat them separately.

It also means he is currently **down about a third** on aerobic load versus the
summer (45 run + ~150 min XT ≈ 60 equivalents, against ~90 in June). Running
volume went up while total aerobic work fell. The cheapest gain available is
putting cross-training back — 45-60 min on easy days, 5 days a week, gets him
to ~78 equivalents without a single extra footfall.

## What the PR set says

| PR | 5K-equivalent |
|---|---|
| 800 — 1:58 | 13:43 |
| 1600 — 4:20 (sick) | 14:30 |
| 3200 — 9:29 (sick) | 15:13 |
| 5K XC — 15:19 | 15:19 |

A 1:36 spread, sloping the same way throughout: the shorter the race, the
better he looks.

The tempting read is "small aerobic engine." The summer block above rules that
out. What cross-training builds is the engine; what it cannot build is the
transmission — running economy at 4:50/mile and the tissue tolerance to absorb
the pounding. That gap explains everything at once: the engine carries him to
15:19, the missing economy leaves the 5K far behind the 800, and the missing
durability is why fast volume increases have injured him three times.

So the sub-15 goal is an economy and durability problem, and the Tuesday workout
should be threshold and sustained work. The Monday ladder that
closed 400 in 59 and 200 in 26 was fun and fast and trained the thing he is
already best at. That is a wasted hard day at this point in the season.

The 3200 is the honest anchor — it converts to 15:13 against an actual 15:19.

## Current fitness

Two independent reads:

- **Same-course delta (stronger).** 17:30 on the opener course last year, 16:59
  this year — 31 seconds faster on identical terrain. Last year's 17:30 sat one
  week before a 15:19, so 16:59 maps to about **14:48**.
- **Course adjustment.** 16:59 minus ~110s for an extremely slow course gives
  about **15:09**.

Call it **14:48–15:09**. And last year's comparison point was a week from peak,
while this year's was his first race back — the whole build is still ahead.

## The tactical note for state

Top 3 is a closing question, not a fitness question. Nobody in a Georgia 6A
field outkicks a 1:58 800 runner. If he is in contact at 400m to go, he wins
that sprint. The job is arriving there in contact and intact.
