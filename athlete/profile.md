## Training history — factor this into every recommendation

**Responds extremely well to cross-training.** Working equivalency is
**10 minutes ≈ 1 mile**. He ran 15:19 off almost pure cross-training, so
cross-training is a real substitute here, not a consolation prize.

**Comes back from layoffs unusually fast.** Fitness has never been the limiter.
Do not panic-build after time off; the fitness returns on its own.

**Gets injured at 60+ miles per week. Three separate times.** This is the single
most important fact in this file. The proven sweet spot is **~50 miles plus hard
workouts plus a cross-training supplement**.

**Injury pattern:** every flare has followed a load or intensity jump, and it
shows up the **next morning**, not during the run. A session that felt fine is
not evidence of anything until the following morning.

**Peaked mid-season last year** instead of at state. This season is deliberately
structured to crest in November.

**Races well on full training. Does not respond to big tapers.** Cut roughly
15% and keep the intensity.

## Standing rules that follow from the above

1. Injury signals override everything else.
2. ~50 mile ceiling; more is earned through consecutive symptom-free weeks.
3. Progression is earned, never scheduled.
4. Never advance volume and intensity in the same week.
5. Two hard running days per week maximum; a race counts as one.
6. No full workout within 48 hours of a race.
7. Grade sessions by next-morning feel.
